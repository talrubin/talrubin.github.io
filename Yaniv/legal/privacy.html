Yaniv — Privacy Policy

Last updated 18 September 2026

Yaniv is a card game published by Tal Rubin. This policy describes exactly what the app stores about you, where it goes, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be checkable rather than comprehensive: every item below corresponds to something specific in the app.

The short version. The app creates an anonymous account for you automatically so your trophies survive a reinstall and so other players can see you in a room. It stores your game progress and settings. It does not ask for your name, email, phone number or location, and it contains no advertising and no third-party trackers. It reports crashes to Unity so they can be fixed, and it collects usage statistics only if you switch them on — see section 7.

1. The account

When you first open Yaniv, the app signs you in to Unity Gaming Services (UGS) as an anonymous guest. This happens automatically, before you tap anything, because the app needs an identity before it can save your progress or seat you in an online room.

That account is an opaque player ID generated by Unity. It is not linked to an email address, a phone number, a Google account or any real-world identity, and Yaniv never asks you for one. Your display name is generated by the app; you are not asked to supply one.

2. What is stored on your device

The following are stored locally, using Android's app-private preference storage. They never leave your device except as part of the cloud sync described in section 3, and the seat credential, which the host of your match issues and which this device sends back to that host to rejoin.

3. What is stored in the cloud

Stored by Unity's Cloud Save service, under your anonymous player ID:

  • Your trophies, games played, games won, and your four settings switches — the same values as above, mirrored so they survive a reinstall or move to a new device.
  • A match resume snapshot (yaniv_match_resume), written once per round only while you are hosting an online match. It holds the state of the match in progress, including every player's cards, keyed by seat rather than by player ID. It is overwritten each round and is not read by anything today.

4. Online play

Online play uses Unity's Lobby and Relay services, and matches are hosted by one of the players rather than by a server we run.

  • Other players in your room can see your display name, how you are doing in the match, and — for as long as you share a room — your anonymous player ID, which the room service lists for every member of the room. The match traffic itself carries a per-match seat token instead of the ID.
  • Room names are typed by whoever creates the room and are visible to anyone who can see that room in the public list. Do not put personal information in a room name.
  • Room membership and the room's game settings exist for as long as the room does, and are deleted when the room closes.
  • Your friend code is a reversible encoding of your anonymous player ID. Anyone you give it to can derive that ID. It is not linked to any personal identity, but treat it as you would a username.

5. Friends

If you use the friends feature, Unity's Friends service stores your friend relationships, any players you have blocked, pending friend requests, and your presence (whether you are online). Adding someone as a friend is visible to them.

6. Notifications

Yaniv can schedule local notifications on your device — a reminder that it is your turn, or that a room is waiting for you. These are scheduled by the app on the device itself. No notification is sent from a server, and no push token is collected.

7. Crash reports and usage statistics

These are two different things with two different answers, so they are described separately.

Crash reports are always on. If the app crashes or hits an error it cannot recover from, Unity's Diagnostics service receives a report so the fault can be found and fixed. A report describes the failure and the machine it happened on: the error and its stack trace, the version of the app, and the device model and operating system version. It does not contain your display name, your player ID, your cards, your room name, or anything you typed.

There is no switch for this inside the app, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise. It is set when the app is built rather than at runtime, so it is not something a setting could turn off. Without it, a crash on your phone leaves no trace anywhere and cannot be fixed. If you would prefer not to send crash reports, uninstalling the app is the way to stop them.

Usage statistics are off unless you turn them on. Under Settings ▸ Account & privacy there is a switch called Share usage data. It starts off. Nothing in this paragraph happens until you turn it on, and turning it off again stops it.

While it is on, Unity's Analytics service receives these four events and nothing else:

EventWhat it carries
match_startedWhether the match is local or online, and how many seats are at the table
match_finishedLocal or online, how many rounds it lasted, and whether you won
room_createdWhether the room you opened has a password
room_joinedHow you reached the room — a share link, the public list, or a typed code

Every value in that table is a number, a yes/no, or one of a fixed set of words. No event carries a name, a room name, a player ID, a friend code, a message, or free text of any kind, and the app has an automated check that fails the build if one ever does. Unity also records its own standard session information — the app version, device model, operating system and a session identifier — while the switch is on.

Turning the switch off stops collection immediately. Delete my data (section 11) also asks Unity to erase everything already collected.

8. What Yaniv does not collect

Stated explicitly, because it is unusually short:

  • No name, email address, phone number or postal address.
  • No location data of any kind, precise or approximate.
  • No contacts, photos, files, microphone or camera access.
  • No payment information — the app has no purchases.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, and no ad networks.
  • No third-party trackers or SDKs beyond the Unity Gaming Services described above. The crash reporting and usage statistics in section 7 are Unity services, not a separate analytics vendor, and neither carries anything that identifies you.

9. Who your data is shared with

Your data is processed by Unity Technologies, which operates the authentication, Cloud Save, Lobby, Relay and Friends services the app is built on. See Unity's privacy policy for game players.

Beyond Unity and the other players in your room, your data is not sold, rented or shared with anyone. There is no advertising partner and no data broker. The crash reports and usage statistics in section 7 go to Unity's own Diagnostics and Analytics services under the same processor relationship as everything else above — there is no third-party analytics vendor.

10. Children

Yaniv is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. Because the app creates only an anonymous account and collects no personal details, there is very little to collect either way. If you believe a child has provided information to us, use the contact address below and we will delete the associated account.

11. Deleting your data

You can delete your account and everything listed above at any time.

  • In the app: tap the settings gear, then Account & privacy, then Delete my data.
  • Without the app: use the data deletion request page. This is the route to use if you have already uninstalled.

Deleting removes the local values in section 2, the Cloud Save records in section 3, your friends and blocks from section 5, any usage statistics already collected under section 7, and the anonymous account itself. It is immediate and cannot be undone: the account is destroyed rather than deactivated, so your trophies do not come back and signing in afterwards creates a brand-new guest.

What can outlive a deletion. If another player was hosting a match you took part in, their own match resume snapshot may still contain a record of that match, including your seat, until it is overwritten by their next match or deleted with their account. We do not retain data for any other reason — there is no backup archive, no fraud-prevention store and no regulatory retention period applied to this app.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, or delete the data an app holds about you, or to object to its processing. The deletion routes in section 11 are self-service and immediate. For anything else, write to the address below.
